<span-block id="b1"/>
In this account, <e ref="e9">patronage</e> appears as both **opportunity and dependence**. <e ref="e1">Leonardo’s</e> move from <e ref="e2">Florence</e> to <e ref="e3">Milan</e> ties artistic work to access to a powerful employer. <e ref="e4">Ludovico’s</e> commission of <e ref="e5">The Last Supper</e> shows a patron helping determine which projects receive support. The mention of <e ref="e6">Cecilia Gallerani</e> broadens the picture of artistic production, although the account does not identify who commissioned her portrait.

<span-block id="b2"/>
<mark id="s1" kind="claim">The conquest of <e ref="e3">Milan</e> by <e ref="e7">French forces</e> under <e ref="e8">Louis XII</e> reveals the arrangement’s vulnerability: political upheaval disrupts both <e ref="e4">Ludovico’s</e> court and <e ref="e1">Leonardo’s</e> employment. <e ref="e9">Patronage</e> therefore connects artistic opportunity to political stability; the same concentration of support that enables work can expose an artist’s livelihood to a patron’s changing fortunes.</mark>
