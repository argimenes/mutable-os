<mark id="s1" kind="qualification">Yes—if vulnerability means difficulty recovering from political upheaval. <e ref="@e1">Leonardo</e> could carry expertise and reputation elsewhere and seek another patron; <e ref="@e2">Ludovico</e> could not carry <e ref="@e3">Milan</e>’s territory, institutions and revenues away with <e ref="@e2">him</e>. But portability did not guarantee employment, payment or safety. <e ref="@e1">Leonardo</e> still depended on access to powerful sponsors, whereas <e ref="@e2">Ludovico</e>, while ruling, commanded resources <e ref="@e1">Leonardo</e> lacked. The distinction is therefore **greater adaptability, not freedom from dependence**: <e ref="@e1">Leonardo</e>’s livelihood was potentially more recoverable, while <e ref="@e2">Ludovico</e>’s power was more tightly bound to a particular political order.</mark>
